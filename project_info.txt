Motivation: While tutoring a visually impaired 7th grade student, I noticed her struggle to comprehend what she was reading in Braille. Her teachers explained the challenges in figuring out how to improve her reading experience. I wanted to figure out a way to help.
Background Research: Fluency in Braille is Essential to Learning → defined by the combination of reading speed, accuracy, and expression, fluency is fundamental for effective understanding of text (Stanfa & Johnson, 2015)
Tactile Sensitivity Impacts Fluency → variance in fingertips textural and tactile perception can impede Braille reading fluency (Martiniello & Wittich, 2022)
Lack of Fluency Measurement Tools → Braille lacks a direct equivalent to tools that help evaluate visual reading fluency, making it difficult for teachers to evaluate the nuanced aspects of tactile reading (Gonzalez-Garcia, 2017)
Cost Limitations of Existing Technologies → While tools like refreshable Braille displays and screen readers exist, they are often expensive, and move the student away from the optimal experience of paper Braille. (Wang et al., 2025a&b)
CONCLUSION: There is currently no low-cost device capable of measuring Braille reading fluency while preserving natural tactile interaction with paper Braille.
