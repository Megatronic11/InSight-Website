The Problem & Motivation

Why InSight Exists

There is currently no low-cost device capable of measuring Braille reading fluency while preserving natural tactile interaction with paper Braille.

The Spark

A student struggling. A problem worth solving.

While tutoring a visually impaired 7th grade student, I noticed her struggle to comprehend what she was reading in Braille.

Her teachers explained the challenges in figuring out how to improve her reading experience. I wanted to figure out a way to help.

"Fluency in Braille is defined by the combination of reading speed, accuracy, and expression, and it is fundamental for effective understanding of text."

Stanfa & Johnson, 2015

"Variance in fingertip textural and tactile perception can impede Braille reading fluency."

Martiniello & Wittich, 2022

"Braille lacks a direct equivalent to tools that help evaluate visual reading fluency, making it difficult for teachers to evaluate the nuanced aspects of tactile reading."

Gonzalez-Garcia, 2017

Background Research

What the research tells us

A review of existing literature revealed four compounding challenges that leave Braille readers underserved.

Fluency is Essential to Learning

Defined by the combination of reading speed, accuracy, and expression, fluency is fundamental for effective understanding of text.

Stanfa & Johnson, 2015
Tactile Sensitivity Impacts Fluency

Variance in fingertip textural and tactile perception can impede Braille reading fluency.

Martiniello & Wittich, 2022
No Fluency Measurement Tools

Braille lacks a direct equivalent to tools that help evaluate visual reading fluency, making it difficult for teachers to evaluate the nuanced aspects of tactile reading.

Gonzalez-Garcia, 2017
Existing Technology Is Cost-Prohibitive

Tools like refreshable Braille displays and screen readers exist, but they are often expensive, and move students away from the optimal experience of paper Braille.

Wang et al., 2025

The Gap

There is currently no solution.

After reviewing the research, one conclusion emerged clearly: there is currently no low-cost device capable of measuring Braille reading fluency while preserving natural tactile interaction with paper Braille.

💡

InSight fills the gap.

A low-cost, paper-compatible fluency tracker that gives teachers real data, without disrupting the student's natural reading experience.

About the Creator

Made by Meghana

The InSight project began while tutoring a visually impaired 7th grade student. After noticing her struggle to comprehend what she was reading in Braille, and hearing her teachers explain the challenges in improving her reading experience, Meghana wanted to figure out a way to help.

The project spans hardware design (CAD modeling in OnShape, wiring an ESP32 microcontroller to a battery pack and potentiometer sensor), firmware programming (Arduino IDE, ESP Async Web Server Library), and data automation (CSV upload to a hotspot webpage, Apps Script graphs in Google Sheets).
