Design Evolution

Three Versions. One Goal.

InSight evolved through three distinct versions — each shaped by student testing, teacher feedback, and a push to give more granular reading insight.

The Versions

From prototype to app

Click any version to see its full breakdown, how it works, and what it taught us.

Version 1
The Line Reader
An ESP-32 with a VL53L1X Time-of-Flight sensor detects the student's finger as it moves across a Braille line. A line blocker guides placement; a start button initiates the session. Speed is measured per full line and uploaded to Google Sheets.
ESP-32 ToF Sensor Line Blocker
See V1 →
Version 2
Refined Design
After testing at the California School for the Blind, the line blocker was removed to preserve natural reading posture. A pressure/linear potentiometer sensor detects end-of-line taps; a non-slip mat keeps the paper stable throughout the session.
Pressure Sensor Non-Slip Mat
See V2 →

Version 3
The App
A phone camera app that captures reading in real time and overlays a color-coded speed heat map on the Braille text. Detects regressions, provides character-level feedback, and gives teachers the most comprehensive reading data yet.

Design Process

How InSight evolved

Version 1
First prototype with ToF sensor

Built an ESP-32 device with a VL53L1X time-of-flight sensor and a line blocker. A start button kicks off each session. Tested with real students for the first time.

Student Testing
California School for the Blind

Testing V1 with students revealed the line blocker disrupted natural reading posture. Paper movement was also identified as an issue.

Version 2
Redesigned around student feedback

Removed the line blocker. Switched to a pressure/linear potentiometer for end-of-line detection. Added a non-slip mat. Students could read more naturally without any physical guide.

Student Testing 2

California School for the Blind and Palo Alto Unified School District

Testing V2 with students revealed that the line by line speed metric was valuable, but didn't capture the full reading experience. Hardware also couldn't capture regressions reliably.

Version 3
Moved to software — phone camera app

Switched from hardware to a phone camera app. Color-coded heat maps, regression detection, and character-level feedback give teachers an entirely new level of reading insight.

🎯

The constant across all versions

Every version of InSight shares the same goal: give teachers real, actionable data about how their students read Braille — without disrupting the student's natural tactile experience on paper Braille.
